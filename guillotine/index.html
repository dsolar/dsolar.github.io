        How the odds work

        This week, each team's expected score is its Yahoo projection. A team with no lineup set gets its best possible lineup instead, built from player projections. Failing both, it gets its average.

        During the week, points already scored are banked exactly: Yahoo's own totals from the latest league paste, then live scoring for games since. Only starters still to play can move a team's score, each by an amount that fits his position (quarterbacks swing least, tight ends most), and nobody still to play is assumed to lose more than 2 points. A team that's done scoring is locked at its total, so the cut odds firm up as the week goes on. The page checks live scoring every minute while games are on.

        Later weeks blend each team's average over its last three weeks with what its current roster projects to, then pull that a quarter of the way toward the league average. Rosters keep changing as cut teams' players go to waivers, so nobody's current form lasts all season.

        The range of outcomes. A projection is a midpoint, not a promise. Weekly scores swing about 22% either way, so a team projected 120 lands between about 86 and 154 in 8 of 10 weeks, and under 86 one week in ten. That's why a 10-point edge isn't safety: a team projected 110 outscores one projected 120 about 4 times in 10.

        The cut line is the median lowest score across all simulations of this week. The safe score is the one that clears the lowest score in 9 of 10 simulations.

        Each simulation plays out the rest of the season, drawing every team's score from that range and cutting the lowest each week until one team is left. The page runs 20,000 of them and counts how often each team gets cut, survives each week and wins.

        Cut-risk colors compare each team with the average risk this week (one in however many teams are left). Danger is at least twice the average, Bubble is above average, and Safe is below it.

        Money left buys the players that cut teams drop. From next week on, each team gets a boost or penalty based on its budget against the average still alive: twice the average is worth about 8 points a week, an empty wallet about 8 fewer, phased in over three weeks as the money gets spent. It never touches this week's odds.
