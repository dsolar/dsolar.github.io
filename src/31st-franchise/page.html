The rules

Picks
14 per team, no more than one player from any NBA team.
Protection
Each NBA team protects up to 8 and exposes the rest. Projected lists.
Eligible
Anyone under contract for 2027-28, restricted free agents and two-way players. Pending unrestricted free agents can't be taken.
Cap
$117.2M, hard. 66.6% of the projected $176M league cap.
Floor
$105.5M. Under it, you pay players the difference.
Two teams
Picks alternate, and each NBA team can lose only one player in total.
Side deals
Teams with the league's worst contracts pay picks for you to take them. A team can also pay you to pass on a young player.
Free agency
After the draft. Sign 2027 free agents into your room under the same cap, up to 15 players. Waived players come off the cap but still get paid.

How you're scored

  1. Every player has an offense and a defense impact rating built from DARKO, RAPM, CraftedNBA and box score numbers. The rating shown is for 2027-28, on a scale where 84 is All-Star level and 90 is All-NBA.
  2. Your depth chart sets minutes: starters 28 to 34, the first five off the bench 10 to 24.
  3. Chemistry adds or takes away points for fit: positions, spacing, usage, playmaking and rim protection.
  4. You play 82 games with injuries and players beating or missing projections.
  5. Your record goes on the leaderboard. Your grade is the average of 1,000 more seasons.

The bar to clear

Every modern expansion team's first season. Nobody has topped the 1966-67 Bulls.

                        How it works

                        Who's eligible. Players under contract for 2027-28, 2023 first-rounders and other players 23 or younger whose deals end after 2026-27 (they'd be restricted free agents), and two-way players. Pending unrestricted free agents are off the board, which is most of why the pool isn't bigger. Each NBA team's protected eight is my projection. Teams always protect their four best players today, unless it's a contract they'd be glad to lose (30 or older and paid $25M+ over his value), plus young players on max deals and franchise stars. After that it's impact, age and contract. The eight weakest rosters are rebuilding: they favor prospects and expose big veteran salaries, which is why Anthony Davis is available and Washington keeps its young players.

                        Ratings. Impact (points per 100 possessions, offense and defense) is a blend of all-in-one metrics: DARKO's DPM (darko.app, through June 3, 2026) 50%, time-decay RAPM (nbarapm.com) 20%, CraftedNBA's CPM where I have it 15%, and 2025-26 box plus/minus 15%. RAPM only publishes its top 100, so anyone outside it is capped at that line. Players who missed most of 2025-26 lean on RAPM and their CraftedNBA history. The big number is the 2027-28 rating the season uses: today's impact aged on Stephen Noh's aging curve. Now is healthy talent today. Both use one fixed scale tied to Noh's impact tiers: MVP level (+6) is 95, All-NBA (+4) 90, All-Star (+2) 84, quality starter (+1) 80, average 72, bench 66, replacement 60. So a 92 means All-NBA, not just top 10%. Pot is where a young player is headed by 27 on the same curve. Tags like Shooter and Rim protector come from measured rate stats.

                        Team rating. Starters play 34, 33, 32, 30 and 28 minutes (best player most), bench spots 1 to 5 play 24, 20, 16, 13 and 10, and spots 6 to 9 don't play. Team rating is the minute-weighted sum of player impact compared with the league, plus chemistry. If a starter is hurt, the best-fitting healthy bench player starts.

                        Positions. A player can start anywhere he logged 15%+ of his 2025-26 minutes, from Basketball-Reference's position estimates, with no penalty. Sam Hauser is a PF who plays SF, so he's PF/SF. Players without that data get a range from their size profile: rebounders slide up, passers slide down. One spot outside the range costs a little, two or more costs a lot.

                        Chemistry. Built from 2025-26 rate stats. Spacing: three or more starters who take 40%+ of their shots from three. Usage: the starters' usage rates should add up to roughly 90 to 112%. Playmaking: someone in the rotation with a 25%+ assist rate. Rim protection: a starting PF or C who blocks 3.5%+ of shots. Plus a backup guard and center, three points off in year one because nobody has played together, players stretched past their usual minutes play worse, and stars count a little extra, and coach fit.

                        The season. 82 games against each NBA team's current roster aged to 2027-28, minus the player it lost. With two expansion teams, they play each other four times. Injuries come in stretches based on how often a player missed time. The top 16 records make a 1-vs-16 bracket of best-of-seven series.

                        Side deals. The dumps are the league's genuinely toxic contracts: Paul George, De'Aaron Fox, Christian Braun, Patrick Williams, Jakob Poeltl and Ja Morant every time, two of Jerami Grant, Dejounte Murray, Darius Garland, Pascal Siakam, Keegan Murray, Myles Turner and Julius Randle, and in about one draft in three the 76ers expose Joel Embiid and protect someone else. Players a team just paid real assets for, like Jaren Jackson Jr., never show up as dumps. The price follows dead money by Stephen Noh's contract model: what he's owed minus what he's worth over the rest of the deal. A one-year deal never brings a first, just one or two seconds. On longer deals, $60M+ of dead money brings two firsts, $35M+ a first and a second, $20M+ a first, $8M+ two seconds, and less than that one second. Firsts come with protections, the way real dumps are written: a weak team protects the lottery, a middling team about the top 10, a good team's late first goes clean, and picks three or more years out carry lighter protection. A protected first that lands inside its protection turns into two seconds, and one with real odds of not conveying comes with an extra second. Every pick is real: one the team actually owns, per RealGM's future pick tracker in October 2026, and could legally trade. That rules out picks already owed, frozen under the second apron or tied up in swap rights, and the Stepien rule means no team goes two straight years without a first. 2027 seconds are in the NBA draft a few days later (only clean ones, not "more favorable of" picks). A team out of seconds pays $3M cash for each one it's missing, up to the league's cash limit of about $9M a team, which is why Phoenix only ever offers cash. Take him instead offers steer you to a bad contract so the team's better player stays home. Pass deals pay you a second, two for a real asset, to leave a good young player alone, and cost you nothing else. A first is worth more the worse its original team projects to be; picks after 2029 are valued as mid-round, and later years are discounted. Your haul shows up under Future assets on the results screen, and each pick adds to your franchise value.

                        Free agency. In real life the expansion draft comes first, then the NBA draft, then free agency opens June 30. An expansion team's year-one cap is 66.6% of the league cap, the same $117.2M, and its floor is 90% of that. You can sign anyone who fits in your room, or anyone at the minimum even over the cap, up to 15 players. The pool is the 2027 unrestricted free agents. A player asks for his market value, 20% more if he's 30 or older (veterans want a contender, not an expansion team) and 10% less if he'd start for you. Each of the four days, other teams sign players, the best ones first, and asking prices drop about 7%. Under the reported expansion rules, a player you waive before the season comes off your cap but still counts toward the floor, and you still pay him, so waiving costs franchise value. The computer GM signs whoever fits best and never waives.

                        The computer GM. Takes the best player it can afford, with a bump for filling an open starting spot and for youth, and a penalty for eating cap it needs later. Pick grades compare your pick with its ranking of everyone you could have taken.

                        Cap math. A pick is blocked if it puts you over $117.2M or leaves too little room to fill the rest of your 14 spots from teams that still have players exposed. If the board runs dry, you sign a minimum free agent, which the minimum exception allows even over the cap.
